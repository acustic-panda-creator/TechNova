Read full problem brief ＋

Problem statement

Cities collect air-quality readings from monitoring stations, satellites, weather services and low-cost sensors, but these sources are often incomplete, inconsistent or geographically sparse. Authorities need to understand not only where pollution is high, but also which sources may be contributing to it and where it is likely to spread. Develop a platform that fuses multi-source environmental data to estimate pollution levels at fine spatial resolution, forecast pollution hotspots and identify likely contributing sources such as traffic, construction or industrial activity.

Technical challenges

  • Combine time-series sensor data, geospatial data, satellite imagery and meteorological features.
  • Handle missing readings, sensor drift, outliers and inconsistent sampling frequencies.
  • Build spatiotemporal forecasting models and uncertainty estimates.
  • Provide interpretable source-attribution estimates instead of unsupported causal claims.
  • Display predicted hotspots and recommended interventions on an interactive GIS map.

Expected deliverable

A working data-ingestion pipeline, forecasting model, map-based dashboard, evaluation against held-out observations and a demonstration of how authorities could use the predictions.
