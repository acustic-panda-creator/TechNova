TTHE STORY BEHIND THE PROTOTYPE

About
TechNova 2026
A unified platform for student builders

Discover why TechNova was created and the vision driving our prototype.

Campus events can rely on scattered PDFs, chat groups and fragmented updates. TechNova 2026 is a hypothetical university hackathon prototype conceived to bring event information into one clear, accessible place—helping students discover challenges, collaborate in teams and explore technology-based solutions.

PROJECT GOALDesign a compact, user-first event website
TARGET USERSStudents, organizers, judges and sponsors
ARCHITECTUREPurpose-built static pages with clear navigation

The origin story

TechNova 2026 begins with a simple observation: university hackathons can encourage digital innovation, but finding the right information about participating is not always straightforward. Schedules, eligibility rules, domains and submission instructions may be spread across posters, documents, social posts and group chats.

This prototype explores a better way to organize that information. Its proposed open-domain, 48-hour hackathon uses guided problem statements and a structured participant journey, while the website acts as a single reference point for the event concept.

The goal is to reduce avoidable confusion so student teams can focus on exploring a problem, developing an idea and presenting a working prototype.

Mission and core values

Four principles guide the event concept and the way its information is presented.

  • 01

    Practical innovation

    Encourage students to move beyond ideas by designing and building prototypes that respond to a clearly defined problem.

  • 02

    Cross-disciplinary teams

    Support collaboration between students with different strengths, from research and interface design to programming and presentation.

  • 03

    Relevant challenge areas

    Invite exploration across sustainability, education technology, healthcare, finance and related technology domains.

  • 04

    Ethical integrity

    Emphasize originality, responsible development, transparent expectations and appropriate attribution. See the Rules page.

Who is TechNova for?

The concept is intended exclusively for current college and university students. It is designed for people interested in applying technology to practical challenges, whether they are drawn to sustainability, education, healthcare, finance or another related field.

Previous hackathon experience is not presented as a requirement in this overview. The central idea is to create a structured opportunity for students to learn from one another and contribute complementary skills.

  • Sustainability
  • Education technology
  • Healthcare
  • Finance
  • Related technology

Why team-based?

The planning concept allows teams of up to six members. Teamwork can bring together skills such as coding, research, design, testing and communication—while giving participants experience of building something collectively.

For the detailed eligibility and team conditions, visit Rules & Eligibility.

The event format

The proposed 48-hour format is organized around five stages. This is an overview, not a detailed timetable.

01 — Registration and domain selection

Students would form a team, select a challenge area and complete the prototype's demonstration registration flow.

02 — Check-in and orientation

Participants would receive the event guidance, rules and expectations for the build and submission process.

03 — Hackathon development

Teams would plan their approach and develop a prototype addressing a selected problem.

04 — Mentorship and checkpoints

Planned check-ins would give teams opportunities to review progress and identify issues while building.

05 — Final project submission

Teams would submit their project for review according to the published submission requirements, if the event were formally established.

Planning note: the event's dates, venue and final operational details have not been confirmed. Check the Schedule page for the prototype's planning information.

The organizing philosophy

In this hypothetical model, a student organizing team would focus on clear communication, consistent event information and a well-structured participant experience. The prototype does not identify a real club or claim institutional authorization.

The proposed team's responsibilities would include:

  • Maintaining a unified set of rules and eligibility information.
  • Coordinating the event stages and mentorship checkpoints.
  • Keeping announcements and website updates accurate and consistent.
  • Preparing clear submission expectations for participants and reviewers.
  • Providing a planned route for participant, judge and sponsor enquiries.

Affiliation and channels

TechNova 2026 is a class project prototype. It is not affiliated with or authorized by a real Bennett University club or society. No official social channels, speakers, judges or sponsors are represented as confirmed.

Team mandate

The organizing identity is illustrative. The site demonstrates how an event team could centralize information rather than presenting a real operating committee.

Intended ecosystem impact

These are goals the prototype is designed to support, not outcomes from an event that has already taken place.

For participants

Hands-on practice, peer collaboration, exposure to mentorship and the opportunity to develop a prototype for a portfolio.

For organizers

A practical exercise in information architecture, user experience, communication and event-planning workflows.

For judges

A more consistent submission structure that could help reviewers compare projects against clearly stated criteria.

For sponsors

A proposed way to introduce industry-relevant challenges and connect with emerging student talent, if sponsors were later confirmed.
